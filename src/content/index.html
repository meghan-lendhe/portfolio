I’m Meghan, a product designer.

I design digital products across education and government services, from playful learning experiences to tools that make complex public-service workflows easier to navigate. I’m also interested in neuroHCI and how neural signals could shape more natural ways to interact with technology.

Explore my UX design work →

I’m exploring the challenges of neural interfaces.

Neural interfaces could make interacting with technology feel more natural. The challenge is understanding what neural signals can reliably tell us, and how to make that useful.

In an earlier project, I co-authored research on detecting emotion and depression from speech using machine-learning and deep-learning models.

I’m currently working on EEG-based intent classification and imagined-speech decoding, and taking part in the EEG/EMG Foundation Challenge at the NeurIPS 2026 Brain & Body Workshop. It explores whether biosignal models can generalize to unfamiliar stimuli, recording sessions, and people.
