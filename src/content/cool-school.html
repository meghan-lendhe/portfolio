Cool School · Game redesign

Reimagining Cool School for a New Generation

Revamping a school conflict-resolution game for children ages 4–7, with a more exploratory and engaging experience designed to stay relevant over time.

Overview

Audience Children ages 4–7
Team Four-person design research team
Focus Kids' game design · Co-design · Game environment
My contributions Competitive research · Co-design planning and facilitation · Research synthesis · Environment assets · Figma prototype

Context

The original Cool School game taught young children how to handle conflicts at school. Players resolved 26 conflicts, each associated with a letter-based trophy from A to Z; resolving them all completed the game.

Developed as a U.S. government initiative in response to school violence in the early 2000s, the game had become dated. Our team set out to refresh the experience for a new generation while preserving its research-backed conflict-resolution learning mechanism.

Design direction

Rather than making conflict resolution the only reason to play, we reimagined the school as a place children could explore, interact, and return to. The learning core stayed in place, while the surrounding game world became more open-ended.

The redesigned experience

The new direction turns a finite sequence of conflict scenarios into a broader school-world experience.

Explore the school

Children can move between and inside school buildings. The environment and its characters offer spontaneous interactions, some connected to conflicts and others simply part of exploring.

Play beyond the conflicts

Mini-games, including playing fetch, add activities around the core learning experience.

Make an avatar their own

Customizable avatars give children a way to express themselves. XP and coins can unlock accessories in an in-game store.

Create reasons to return

Daily achievements and an open-ended number of conflicts move the game beyond its original fixed A-to-Z completion structure.

Throughout these changes, we retained the original conflict-resolution learning mechanism, which was grounded in extensive research.

Process & contribution

  1. Research kids' games

    I researched game-design patterns in children's games to inform the redesign direction.

  2. Co-design with children

    I planned and facilitated co-design sessions with the KidsTeam co-design group, then synthesized insights from those sessions and expert feedback.

  3. Bring the school world to life

    I designed assets for the game environment and created the Figma prototype to communicate the redesigned experience.

Reflection

This redesign explored how the game could feel more open-ended and inviting without losing sight of what it was built to teach. The work combined children's perspectives, expert feedback, and familiar game patterns with a refreshed visual direction.
